DuskRead

The app, and the design system behind it

A reading list, personalised to how you actually read

Kept automatically by Claude and a Notion database, not by you. Claude files what's worth keeping out of Gmail; the blogs you follow pull their own new posts. What surfaces on Home is ranked against your own reading habit — the sources you return to, the topics you finish — not whatever landed last.

Two schemes. Paper Black, the one you are looking at, is a near-black ground with a single terracotta accent lit, and that accent means one thing: there is sound here. Ink is the same layout with the hue drained out entirely, leaving lightness, weight and spacing to separate one element from another. The switch in the corner repaints every screen below — worth flipping once.

Act one · Concept

One shape, one word, and the hour it is named for

What the app is called, and where the name comes from. What it does is the act below.

DuskRead

dev.mks.duskread

The name

Two halves that each do a job

Dusk is the hour it is for — you find something good at eleven in the morning and you are not going to read it at eleven in the morning. Read is deliberately both senses: read it, or have it read to you.

What it does

Four things, and it has never wanted a fifth

Every screen in the act below is one of these four, or the way you get between them.

Saved links

A pasted or shared URL is in the list before its title has finished loading — the same list Notion is quietly filing newsletters into on its own. Pasting by hand still works; it simply isn't what carries the habit any more.

Feeds

Follow a blog and its posts land beside the links you saved yourself, in the same one list rather than in a second inbox to keep up with.

Readback

Some pieces are better heard than read. Audio plays from the row it belongs to rather than from a player you have to go and find, and it is the one thing the accent is ever spent on.

Focus

A timer wrapped around the reading rather than sitting beside it. The pile was never the hard part; sitting down with it is.

01 / 07  ·  Reading

Read it here, or ask what is in it

The reader view strips the page to its text. From there the summary button hands that same extracted text to Gemini Nano, on the phone — nothing uploaded, no connection needed.

The panel floats over the article and nothing else on screen moves. Checking a summary never costs your place in the thing you were reading.

Prose, not bullets. Fragments ask you to reassemble the argument you opened the panel to avoid. One paragraph, one tone. The button is hidden rather than greyed out until the reader has text to give it.

02 / 07  ·  Home

What is there to read, without going to look

Four sections, in this order: next up, whatever is mid-readback, the focus presets, and the following digest. Everything the app knows, on one screen, before you have chosen a tab.

Next up is the only one that makes a choice rather than reporting state, and it chooses over both halves of the app: every unread saved link and every cached post from a followed blog, one pool. A hero and two runners-up — one row reads as a decree, a list reads as a browse.

Flush on the background, separated by their own hairline. No cards, and no horizontal strip of tiles nested inside a section. Two eyebrows carry a glyph rather than text alone, so a running timer is legible from the label.

Following goes last. It is the only section that can be arbitrarily long, so growing costs nothing beneath it — and two lines and a count are enough to decide whether the screen on the right is worth opening.

03 / 07  ·  Following

A digest line per blog, opened in place

The dedicated screen behind Home’s digest. One line per feed: host, how many posts you have not saved, a chevron. The count is the only accented thing, and only while there is something behind it.

Expanded, each post carries a bookmark. Tapping it is what moves the post into Saved; swiping it right asks for a summary instead. The row itself opens the article.

The bookmark is the only way out. A feed post reaches Saved by being bookmarked here and no other way — and it is two glyphs, not one glyph in two colours, because filled reads as “saved” at 14dp in a way a tint change does not, and it is the only signal that survives Ink.

Three, then a door. Tapping expands the newest three as the same flat rows the full screen is built from, then hands over — so one section of Home cannot push everything under it off the bottom.

04 / 07  ·  Saved

Unread and read, told apart without a heading

A link arrives by paste or by the share sheet and is in the list immediately, backfilling its real title when the fetch lands. Never blocked on the network.

A read link recedes rather than being struck through — one alpha over the whole row, plus a trailing tick. Read is the one section label that stays muted: it sits under an unread list and should not compete with it.

05 / 07  ·  Settings

Two pasted values, no OAuth screen

A personal access token and a database ID, pasted once each — Notion only ever shows a token at the moment it is created, so there is nothing to look up a second time. A second, optional database ID keeps a separate reading list in step the same way.

Test connection checks the token without touching a feed; Sync now pulls whatever changed since the last run.

Disconnect stays out of the accent. Every other action on this row is reversible; the one that is not never gets the colour that means “do this” everywhere else on the screen.

06 / 07  ·  Focus

A meter, not a word in a void

Pick a length, put the phone down, read the thing. The countdown is drawn with the same bars as every waveform in the app — elapsed filled, remaining not — so a bar two-thirds lit means the same thing on both screens.

Bottom-anchored in the thumb zone rather than centred, and glanceable from a metre away. A finished session alerts on its own high-importance channel: the per-second countdown’s has to stay silent, so completion needed a second one.

07 / 07  ·  Readback

Where the accent earns its keep

Audio a sync step has already put on the device, from the separate readback project, read-only. Two facts per row — the third, how long this is, is drawn as a waveform.

The playing row is the only coloured thing on screen, and the floating bar has given its width over to the transport.

The tip above the list points at that bar rather than repeating it: the controls stay where the thumb already is, whatever the list is doing.

One pill, three faces. The transport lives inside the floating bar rather than on a second pill above it — two stacked slabs ate a third of the reachable zone. Playback wins the bar while it is running; the trailing icon is the tab you are already on, so it reads as “back to where you were” rather than a fourth destination.

01 / 06  ·  Monochrome

Ink — the default, not a colour stripped away

Hue drops to nothing and level is luminance alone. It is the app’s own default; this page opens in Paper Black because a landing page has to be looked at first.

Paper Black is one tap on top of that — the contrast toggle at the end of the floating bar, or Settings — for a reader who wants the colour. This frame is pinned to Ink regardless of the switch in the corner; every other mockup on the page follows it.

One tap adds the accent, nothing else moves. The reader button is the one coloured thing on this screen — active means “this is the view you’re in,” and that ring is terracotta in Paper Black, white here. Reading itself was never waiting on colour to work.

02 / 06  ·  Colour

One accent, and it is only ever spent on sound

Terracotta means one thing: there is sound here. Nothing else in the interface is ever allowed to be coloured.

Below it, eight steps from the ground to the ink — and the only thing separating one from the next is lightness. Both schemes run the same ladder; Ink simply has nothing else.

Every value comes from ui/theme/Theme.kt: a hard-coded Color(0x…) in a screen survives the swap to Ink and immediately looks wrong. The full table is in design-tokens.md.

03 / 06  ·  Type

Jost and Inconsolata, five roles

Jost for everything the eye reads as language, Inconsolata for every number and meta line, so data never borrows the platform’s default mono.

Four static Jost weights and no light cut: at these sizes, on a near-black ground, a light weight disappears.

The section label is the one that gets confused. Uppercase and tightly tracked, so it reads as mono at a glance — but it is Jost. Inconsolata marks data: a duration, a word count, a host.

04 / 06  ·  Icons

Bar — the waveform is the alphabet

All twenty-two, drawn from DuskReadIcons.kt’s own path data. One spec throughout: 2.4 weight, round cap and join, nothing filled.

Every glyph that can be built from evenly spaced vertical bars is, so the set agrees with the data on screen rather than merely sitting beside it.

BookmarkFilled is the single filled glyph, and only the on half of a pair. Two states have to be told apart at icon size, and a tint change is a colour difference — the one thing Ink does not have.

05 / 06  ·  App icon

One mark, in Ink, everywhere it appears

The house rule — that the waveform is the alphabet — carries into the launcher icon too: one wide bar, with a crescent bitten from its top for dusk.

The terracotta launcher pair it used to swap between at runtime is gone. An icon that changes colour behind the user’s back is a bug report waiting to happen.

The bite is painted, not subtracted. The circle overruns the bar on two sides, so a hole that runs outside its own shape is not a hole. The splash that launches it is a launch theme, not a composable — up before the first frame, its ground the same #161616 as the tile and the app’s own background.

06 / 06  ·  Shape and motion

Values that carry a decision, not a measurement

Nothing in the app is fully round except an actual circular icon button. Each radius is one step tighter than the surface holding it, so a row never competes with its panel.

Navigation and the visualiser move at deliberately different speeds: navigation should feel immediate, whereas a tone change is teaching and has to be slow enough to follow.

An optical one-off stays a literal at its call site. Padding of top = 14.dp, bottom = 13.dp is a nudge to make one label sit right, not a rule, and naming it would imply a system that is not there.

Act four · Get it

One clone, one Gradle task

Android is the app, built and tested on a phone day to day. Saved links, feeds, the timer and both schemes work the moment it opens.

git clone https://github.com/MKS-01/duskread.git && cd duskread
./gradlew :androidApp:installDebug  # ~5s once warm
Android12 and up. The target that is finished.
SummariesNeed a phone with AICore — Pixel 9 and up, Galaxy S24 and up, and similar. Everywhere else the control simply is not drawn.
ReadbackAudio comes from the separate readback project, synced onto the device. Without it the tab is empty rather than hidden.
Desktop, web, iOSBuild from the same source and exist to prove it travels. The workshop, not the product.
